Chord shapes

The chord picker shows how a chord is played. Build the chord you want, flip through its shapes and listen before you insert it.

On this page
  • Build a chord
  • Add a bass note (slash chord)
  • Browse shapes and hear them
  • Switch between guitar and piano
  • Go full screen
  • Find a chord by tapping frets
  • Left-handed players

Build a chord

  1. On the Chords tab, tap a word (or tap an existing chord pill) to open the picker.
  2. Choose a Root, then an accidental, Quality, Modifier and Extension.

The big name at the top updates as you choose, and the diagram redraws.

Options that do not make musical sense are greyed out. A power chord such as A5 takes no extension or sus.

Add a bass note (slash chord)

  1. In the picker, open the Bass menu below the option rows.
  2. Choose the bass note.

A C chord with G in the bass reads C/G.

Browse shapes and hear them

The counter under the diagram, such as 3 of 6, shows which shape you are looking at. Step through the others with the arrows beside it.

Switch between guitar and piano

Tap Piano at the top of the picker to see the chord as lit-up keys, and Guitar to go back.

Go full screen

  1. Tap the expand button (two diagonal arrows) beside the shape counter.
  2. Step through shapes with the arrows, or tap the speaker button on the piano to hear it.

Find a chord by tapping frets

  1. Open the full-screen neck and switch to the hand-tap (Detect) mode, the last segment beside the eye.
  2. Tap the frets you are holding down.
  3. Tap one of the chord chips that appears, then tap the check mark.

Heads-up

You can only tap fret 1 and above, and strings you leave untapped count as silent. Open strings cannot be marked, so open shapes like G or Em will not be recognized. Shapes made of fretted notes work best.

Left-handed players

Turn on Left-handed fretboard in Settings, under Editor. Every diagram mirrors, with the nut on the right.
